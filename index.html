Project Overview

Plants, People and Mountain Landscapes

This digital portfolio brings together photographs from field activities, high-altitude medicinal plant documentation, Brokpa cultural life, festivals, and conservation-linked landscapes of Tawang.

High-altitude medicinal plants

Tawang’s alpine and sub-alpine ecosystems support medicinal plant diversity adapted to cold, wind, short growing seasons, and fragile mountain habitats.

Women-led field work

Women workers are central to field-level conservation, nursery management, planting, weeding, maintenance, and day-to-day care of medicinal plant resources.

Culture and traditions

Brokpa pastoral life, local festivals, and traditional relationships with landscapes add cultural depth to conservation storytelling in Tawang.

Women-led Conservation in Action

This section gives special emphasis to women workers engaged in field activities, medicinal plant cultivation, nursery management, and conservation work in Tawang.

Women at the Heart of Field Conservation

The conservation of high-altitude medicinal plants is not only a scientific task. It is also sustained by the regular labour, care, observation, and skill of women workers who maintain nurseries, prepare plots, support planting, and protect young plants in challenging mountain conditions.

Culture and Traditions · 19 Photos

Brokpa Life, Festivals and Living Landscapes

This section presents Brokpa photographs, festival moments, cultural identity, pastoral life, and the relationship between people, animals, plants, and high-altitude landscapes.

Culture as Conservation Storytelling

In Tawang, alpine landscapes are not only biological spaces. They are cultural landscapes shaped by pastoral movement, festivals, local identity, traditional knowledge, and community relationships with plants and animals.

By including Brokpa life and festival photographs, this portfolio connects medicinal plant conservation with the human stories that make these landscapes meaningful.
