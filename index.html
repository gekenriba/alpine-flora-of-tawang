Outreach & Research

Extension Work

Bridging scientific knowledge and community practice — bringing alpine plant wisdom to farmers, conservationists, and local communities.

🌱

Community Nurseries

Establishing village-level alpine plant nurseries that preserve rare species and provide sustainable livelihoods for highland communities, focusing on medicinal and culturally significant plants.

📚

Ethnobotany Workshops

Regular workshops combining traditional ecological knowledge with modern botanical science, documenting local plant use and training the next generation of citizen scientists.

🗺️

Mapping & Surveys

GPS-aided vegetation surveys across altitude gradients, building the first comprehensive digital atlas of alpine flora distribution changes due to shifting climate patterns.

💊

Medicinal Plant Trails

Developing interpretive trails that educate visitors on the medicinal properties of alpine plants, generating eco-tourism revenue that funds conservation directly.

🤝

Farmer Field Schools

Hands-on learning programs where highland farmers learn sustainable harvesting, value-added processing, and market linkages for alpine plant products.

🌍

Seed Conservation

Operating a community seed bank protecting over 200 alpine plant varieties, with participatory seed exchange networks spanning three mountain districts.

Heritage & Practice

Culture & Traditions

Plants as Living Memory

For highland communities, alpine plants are far more than vegetation — they are calendars, medicines, rituals, and stories. The blooming of Edelweiss marks the mid-summer festival season; the first Gentian signals it is time to take livestock to high pastures.

"When the snowbell flowers open, the mountain is ready to receive us."
— Traditional proverb, Himalayan highlands

Generations of traditional ecological knowledge have been passed down through oral traditions, weaving plant lore into songs, naming conventions for landscapes, and seasonal ceremonies that align human activity with the rhythm of alpine ecosystems.

Our work documents these living connections before they are lost — not as museum pieces, but as dynamic knowledge systems that continue to guide sustainable relationships between people and mountain environments.

🌿 Medicinal Heritage

Over 80 alpine species are used in traditional medicine systems, treating ailments from altitude sickness to rheumatism. Healers known as amchi maintain complex knowledge of plant combinations, preparation methods, and seasonal potency.

🎨 Textile & Dye Plants

Plants like Alpine Lady's Mantle and Mountain Sorrel yield rich yellows and greens used in traditional textile dyeing. Each color carries cultural significance in ceremonial weavings and festival garments.

🏔️ Sacred Landscapes

Certain alpine plant communities are protected by customary law as sacred groves. The flowering of specific species triggers community gatherings, offerings, and protocols that have served as informal conservation for centuries.

🍽️ Food & Ritual

Wild Garlic, Wood Sorrel, and Alpine Strawberry feature in traditional highland cuisine. Harvesting is governed by reciprocal protocols — never taking more than a third, always offering back seeds or thanks.

📖 Oral Knowledge Systems

Plant names in local languages encode habitat, season, use, and relationship to other species — a compressed taxonomy passed through generations of careful observation, now being digitally archived with community consent.

🌸 Floral Festivals

Annual flower festivals celebrate the return of alpine blooms after winter, with communities competing in floral arrangement, sharing preserved botanical knowledge, and honoring elders who are living encyclopedias of plant lore.
