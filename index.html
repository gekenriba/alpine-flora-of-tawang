Project Overview

Plants, People and Mountain Landscapes

Alpine Flora of Tawang is a visual and research-oriented digital portfolio documenting the biological richness, cultural depth, and conservation significance of the high-altitude landscapes of Tawang.

The Tawang region of Arunachal Pradesh forms part of the Eastern Himalayan landscape, one of the most ecologically sensitive and culturally rich mountain systems in India. Its alpine and sub-alpine habitats support a remarkable diversity of medicinal plants that have adapted to harsh environmental conditions such as low temperature, short growing seasons, high wind exposure, and fragile mountain soils.

Many of these high-altitude medicinal plant species hold immense ecological, cultural, and livelihood value. They are connected not only to traditional healing systems and local knowledge, but also to biodiversity conservation, climate resilience, and sustainable mountain development. However, these plant resources are increasingly threatened by climate change, habitat degradation, unsustainable harvesting, land-use changes, and declining natural regeneration in fragile alpine ecosystems.

This website brings together field documentation, medicinal plant photographs, women-led conservation activities, Monpa traditions, Brokpa pastoral life, and festival-linked cultural landscapes. It aims to communicate that conservation in the Himalayas is not only about protecting species, but also about understanding the relationship between plants, people, climate, culture, and living landscapes.

Field Work

Women-led Conservation in Action

Women from the Tawang region are playing a vital role in the conservation of high-altitude medicinal plant species through a strong community-based collective approach, particularly in the face of increasing climate change pressures on fragile Himalayan ecosystems. Working in collaboration with village communities, Self-Help Groups (SHGs), and concerned government departments such as the State Horticulture Research and Development Institute (SHRDI) and the Forest Department, they actively participate in the protection, cultivation, monitoring, and sustainable management of these ecologically and culturally significant plant resources. Their involvement not only strengthens biodiversity conservation and climate resilience efforts but also promotes community stewardship, preservation of traditional ecological knowledge, and sustainable livelihood opportunities for local populations.

Medicinal Plant Gallery

High-Altitude Medicinal Plants

The Tawang region is endowed with a rich diversity of high-altitude flora, including several of the Himalaya’s most valuable medicinal plant species. However, these ecologically significant plant resources are increasingly threatened by the impacts of climate change, habitat degradation, and growing anthropogenic pressures such as unsustainable harvesting and land-use changes. Immediate and scientifically informed conservation measures are therefore essential to safeguard the extremely fragile alpine ecosystems of the Tawang region and other Himalayan landscapes. Strengthening conservation initiatives, promoting sustainable management practices, and enhancing community participation are crucial for preserving the unique biodiversity and ecological stability of these high-altitude environments for future generations.

Culture and Traditions

Monpa Traditions and the Brokpa Way of Life

The Monpas are one of the major indigenous communities of the Eastern Himalayas, inhabiting the high-altitude regions of Tawang, Arunachal Pradesh. Among them, the Brokpas meaning “people of the high pastures” are traditionally yak-herding communities whose lives are deeply connected to the alpine landscapes. Their festivals, traditional lifestyles, spiritual practices, and indigenous knowledge reflect a unique harmony between culture, biodiversity, and the fragile Himalayan environment.
